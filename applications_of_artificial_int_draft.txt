ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly transforming higher education through adaptive learning systems, intelligent tutoring agents, and predictive student analytics. While these innovations offer unprecedented potential to tailor educational materials to individualized student paces and cognitive needs, they simultaneously introduce critical concerns regarding data privacy, algorithmic fairness, and equitable access. This paper examines the operational efficacy of AI tools in supporting personalized learning, focusing on the systemic challenges institutions face when deploying automated instructional systems. Drawing upon a mixed-methods evaluation of undergraduate STEM cohorts and learning analytics data, this study analyzes how adaptive pacing mechanisms influence student concept retention and academic self-efficacy. Furthermore, the paper investigates systemic vulnerabilities in assessment algorithms that risk perpetuating historical demographic disparities. The anticipated findings indicate that while adaptive AI feedback boosts self-directed learning gains by quantifiable margins, uncalibrated predictive systems risk reinforcing achievement gaps among underrepresented student populations. Ultimately, this research proposes an actionable institutional framework that integrates ethical algorithmic audits, transparent student consent protocols, and human-in-the-loop pedagogies to cultivate an equitable, technology-augmented learning environment.
